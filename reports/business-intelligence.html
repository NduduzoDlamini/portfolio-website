01 · Executive Summary

Executive Summary

Dataset Overview This dataset covers 1,026 individuals across Europe, North America, and the Pacific region, capturing demographic, financial, and lifestyle attributes to understand what drives bike purchasing decisions. Overall Purchase Rate Nearly half the population surveyed — 48.2% (495 individuals) — purchased a bike, indicating a strong and addressable market. The average customer is 44 years old with an annual income of $56,209.

Key Drivers of Purchase Commute Distance is a strong predictor. Short-distance commuters (0–2 miles) show the highest purchase rates (55–59%), while those commuting 10+ miles are the least likely to buy (29%). This suggests bikes are primarily seen as a short-commute solution. Income & Occupation matter. High-income segments like Management and Professionals show purchase rates as high as 70.6% in certain demographic groups, while lower-income Manual workers in Europe hover around 35–55%. Gender & Marital Status play a role. Single males with a Bachelor's degree in Management (North America) and single females in Skilled Manual roles both reached 70.6% purchase rates — the highest observed segments.

Rather than treating the database as a collection of isolated records, the project uses SQL to establish relationships, identify patterns and surface areas that warrant further investigation. The resulting analysis provides a foundation for understanding operational performance, user activity and changes in behaviour across reporting periods.

Data Quality Profile records, identify missing values and investigate anomalies before drawing conclusions.
Operational Analysis Measure activity and compare performance across relevant stations, periods or operational dimensions.
Trend Analysis Use window functions and period comparisons to evaluate changes in user activity and retention.
